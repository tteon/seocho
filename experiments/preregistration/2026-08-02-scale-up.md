# Pre-registration — scaling the verification study

Written and committed before the run starts. Nothing below has been measured at
this sample size.

    scale       280 cases, 35 per category across all eight
    condition   A only, no ontology
    models      DeepSeek-V3.1, gpt-oss-120b, MiniMax-M2.7
    volume      840 extractions, ~4,200 model calls at the measured 5.0 per
                case per model. DeepSeek's 1,500-per-day ceiling is the binding
                constraint and lands at ~1,400
    tag         s1, isolating databases and workspaces from both earlier sweeps

## Why this run exists

The verification measurement at 16 cases had between three and six
answer-relevant facts per condition. Nothing can be concluded from that, in
either direction, and saying so is not the same as saying verification failed.
Twenty times the sample should put the count near a hundred, which is enough for
an interval.

## Why one condition, and why the one that is not ours

Part 1 already settled the ontology question with separated intervals: no
ontology beats every ontology condition on cross-view agreement. Re-running all
four at scale would spend the budget re-confirming a finished result.

The condition kept is the no-ontology baseline. It produced the highest
cross-view agreement, so it gives verification the most material to work with —
and it is the condition least flattering to the schema work this project has
been doing. If verification still fails there, it fails on the most favourable
ground available. If it succeeds, nobody can say the schema was chosen to help.

## Hypotheses

**H1 — the shortage is a sample-size problem.** Answer-relevant verifiable facts
should scale roughly with cases, from six at n=16 to around a hundred at n=280.
*Disconfirmed if* the count grows sublinearly, which would mean the shortage is
structural — that most facts are held by one view no matter how many cases are
added — and no amount of extra data fixes it.

**H2 — checking agreement improves what gets served.** Among answer-relevant
facts, serving only when views agree should have higher precision than serving
the first view's figure, at some cost in recall.
*Disconfirmed if* the precision difference has an interval crossing zero, or if
recall falls more than precision rises on any sensible weighting.

**H3 — the ceiling does not move.** The share of facts held by exactly one view
should stay near where it is now, around 95%. This is the pre-registered
expectation of *no* improvement, and it is the one most likely to hold.
*Disconfirmed if* the single-view share falls materially with scale.

H1 and H3 are close to opposites in their consequences and both are stated on
purpose. If H3 holds and H1 fails, the limiting result stands and the method
paper does not: verification would have too little to rule on regardless of
sample. If H1 holds, H2 becomes answerable for the first time.

## What will not be claimed

One condition, so nothing here speaks to the ontology again. Correctness remains
"the figure appears in the gold answer", which counts a right number attached to
the wrong entity as correct, and scores facts rather than answers. Cases are
drawn balanced across categories, which does not match the corpus's own
proportions, so category-level rates are not population estimates.

The 16 cases already run are not excluded from the draw. Where one is redrawn it
is re-extracted under the new tag rather than reused, because a resumed partial
from a different sweep would silently mix two runs.

## Addendum, written while the run is at 115 of 840

The hypotheses above were written before the provenance-keying result existed
and they frame the shortage of verifiable facts as a sample-size problem. That
framing is now incomplete, and saying so after the results land would be
retrofitting.

At sixteen cases, matching facts by their anchor in the source rather than by
name took condition C from 30 comparable pairs to 183, and from 2 detected
disagreements to 87. So there is a third possible outcome the original H1/H3
pair cannot express:

**H4 — the shortage was a keying problem, not a sample problem.** The count of
answer-relevant verifiable facts stays low under name keying even at 280 cases,
and rises sharply under anchor keying at the same sample.
*Disconfirmed if* anchor keying's advantage shrinks with scale, which would mean
the sixteen-case result was a small-sample artefact.

H4 competing with H1 changes what the run decides. Under H1 the method paper
needs more data. Under H4 it needs a different key and the data it already has.
Both will be reported against, including the case where neither holds and the
counts simply stay low under both keys — which would leave the limiting result
standing and is still a possible outcome.

One analysis is added to the fixed list for this:

    python3 experiments/materialize_anchors.py           --tag s1
    python3 experiments/minimal/provenance_keying.py     --tag s1 --arms A

Nothing already stated is withdrawn. H1, H2 and H3 are scored as written.
